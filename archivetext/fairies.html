## Flower Fairies of Xis: a reference book All fairies (archaic: faerie) fall under the genus Nymph, which encompasses three species, not including fae (alternatively spelt fay, not to be confused with genus fey). Fairies can be found on four different continents (Orie Eoso excluded) and most islands, with the largest population density in Cedar, Xis. Fairies range in size, from inch-tall dwarf air variants to garden fairies averaging five feet. Contrary to popular belief, fairies did not originate in Cedar. They are thought to have come from Opella and migrated after the arrival of anth, namely selkies. Despite historic genus hostilities, a robust fairy population still resides in Opella today.![world map](../art/fairymap.png width="400" class="pixel") This reference book focuses on earth classification fairies, more specifically garden varieties. Garden fairies are the most common fairy variant, with hundreds of specializations existing. Despite being the largest garden specialization, flower fairies are depicted rarely in pop culture, overshadowed by the larger garden fairies who often participate in anth and sapien society. In contrast, flower fairies usually form communities amongst themselves even when within the realm of a larger mixed society. Often referred to as “a garden’s friendly foe,” flower fairies are given by the larger sapien-anth world as much attention and autonomy as the beetles flower fairies keep as pets. This reference book aims to help the layperson not only identify but possibly form a connection to fairies in their own backyard. Flower fairies are unique because of their resemblance to distinct local flowers as a method of camouflage against predators. Despite common misconceptions, their flowery accoutrements, called tissu, are neither clothing nor body parts. Tissu grows naturally throughout a fairy’s lifetime. It is made primarily of nitin (a substance similar to keratin) and occasionally threaded with thin nerve bundles. With the development of the loom and recent attempts to integrate disregarded species into mainstream anth-sapien society, “false-tissu,” clothing mimicking natural nitin structures, has been popularized. 13% of flower fairies outside Xis, and 35% inside, wear accessories and/of false-tissu for social or migration reasons. This hearkens back to the old fairy tradition of “disguises” made of real petals and leaves and adopted in times of stress. Aside from migration false-tissu, today’s false-tissu is worn merely for leisure.
